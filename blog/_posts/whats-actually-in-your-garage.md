# What's Actually in Your Garage

Most people can tell you roughly what's in their garage, and most people are wrong by about a third.

It's not a memory problem. It's that a garage fills up in layers over ten or fifteen years, and nobody ever sees the whole thing at once. You see the front. You see the path to the car. The back wall is a rumour.

This is a short, honest guide to what's usually back there — because in the collections we buy, the good stuff is almost never the stuff the owner remembered.

## The five places it hides

**The back wall, behind everything.** Whatever went in first. If you moved in with boxes and stacked them at the back, that's the oldest material in the building and statistically the most interesting.

**The shelf you need a ladder for.** Small, light things go up high — which is exactly the profile of cartridges, handhelds, controllers and boxed figures. High shelves are disproportionately valuable per square foot.

**The tub with somebody else's handwriting on it.** Inherited from a parent, a grandparent, a house clearance. These are the ones that surprise people most often, because you never chose what went in.

**The corner where the kids' stuff went.** When children outgrow something it doesn't get sold, it gets relocated to the garage in one afternoon and then stays. Whole bins of figures and playsets end up here in one move.

**The "sort this out later" pile.** Every garage has one. It has been called that for years.

## What we're actually looking for in there

You don't need to identify any of this — this is just so you know whether it's worth a photo:

- **Consoles of any era**, working or not, boxed or loose, missing cables or complete
- **Handhelds**, including the ones with the screen scratched to death
- **Loose cartridges and discs** — including piles of common ones
- **Controllers, cables, memory cards, adapters** — the accessory drawer counts
- **Action figures and playsets**, loose or boxed, complete or missing pieces
- **Vintage toy lines**, including ones that look thoroughly played with
- **Whole bins of mixed stuff** where you don't know what half of it is

That last one is the most common thing we buy, for what it's worth.

## The three things people wrongly assume disqualify something

**"It's not in the box."** Boxes help. Their absence doesn't disqualify anything. Most of what we buy is loose.

**"It doesn't work."** Non-working hardware still has value — parts, repair, and in some cases the item is worth having regardless. Tell us it doesn't work and we'll price it as not working. Don't throw it out on that basis.

**"It's just the common stuff."** Volume has its own value, and "common" is frequently wrong. The things people are certain are worthless are the things that most often surprise them.
