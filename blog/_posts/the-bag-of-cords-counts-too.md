# The Bag Of Cords Counts Too

Somewhere in your house — a drawer, a closet shelf, a grocery bag inside another grocery bag — is the accessories graveyard. Controllers with the ribbed cords. Wall adapters for machines you no longer own. Cables with connectors nobody can name anymore, coiled together so thoroughly they've fused into a single organism. If it's labeled at all, the label says something like "misc???", and the question marks are doing honest work.

Here's the part people never believe: we want to see that bag.

## Why the mystery bag has value

Nothing about a loose cord announces what it belongs to — and that's exactly why the bag is worth a look. Accessories didn't survive the decades at the same rate as the machines they served. Consoles got kept for sentiment; their controllers, adapters, and cables got scattered into drawers, lost in moves, or thrown out in exactly this kind of bag by people doing exactly the cleanout you're considering. Which means the boring bag is often the scarce half of somebody's setup.

That anonymous black power brick? There's a collector somewhere whose console works without it only in theory. The off-brand controller with the strange ergonomics your cousin swore by? Still the missing piece of a lot of childhood setups. The memory card that's been in the bottom of the bag since the second Bush administration? That one's practically a headline. Loose accessories are the ingredient that turns somebody's incomplete console into a complete one — and complete is where the value lives.

## Don't untangle it

We say this about everything, but it goes double here: don't sort the bag. Don't untangle the organism. Don't try to match cords to consoles — that's a research project, it's frustrating, and it's genuinely our job. Don't throw out the ones that "look like junk," because you'd be filtering with the least information anyone in this transaction has. The adapter that looks most like garbage is routinely the one that matters.

And don't test anything. No plugging mystery adapters into things to see what happens — that's how mystery adapters earn their reputation. Condition unknowns are baked into how we look at a bag like this.

## The whole process

Open the bag so the contents are visible. Take one photo from above, in decent light. Send it. That's the entire ask — no inventory, no untangling, no drive across town to find out if it was worth asking.

We'll tell you what we're seeing in there, and come back with a real cash number. If it works for you: OKC metro, we pick up or meet, cash, receipt, done. The drawer gets to be a drawer again.

The consoles get the glory. The bag of cords is where the setups get completed — and yes, it counts too.
