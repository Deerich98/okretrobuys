# You Don't Have to Sort It First

The number one reason people put off selling us a collection isn't price. It's the sorting.

There's a bin in the garage, or a closet with three shoeboxes in it, and somewhere in your head is a job: pull it all out, figure out what's what, look up which ones are worth something, separate the good from the junk, *then* message somebody. That job is why the bin has been there since 2019.

Here's the thing we'd like you to know: that job isn't yours. It's ours. And we'd genuinely rather you didn't do it.

## Why we'd rather see it unsorted

**Because "junk" is the wrong instinct about half the time.** The stuff people set aside as worthless is regularly the stuff we're most interested in — a scuffed loose cartridge of something uncommon, an accessory nobody remembers being rare, the box that everybody threw away. Meanwhile the thing that looks impressive on the shelf is often the one that was printed by the million. Sorting by gut feel usually removes the wrong pile.

**Because loose and incomplete are still worth money.** No box, no manual, missing a cord, one controller instead of four — none of that disqualifies anything. It changes the number, not the answer.

**Because the sorting is the whole reason it hasn't happened.** Every week we talk to somebody who's been meaning to do this for two years. The bin doesn't need organizing. It needs a photograph.

## What we actually need from you

One photo. Genuinely.

Open the bin, hold the phone above it, take the picture. If it's a closet shelf, photograph the shelf. If it's four boxes, photograph the four boxes. You don't need good light, you don't need to lay anything out, and you don't need to know what any of it is.

From that photo we can usually tell you three things straight away: whether it's the sort of thing we buy, roughly what range we're looking at, and what — if anything — we'd want a closer look at.

If something in the pile needs a second photo, we'll ask for that specific thing. That's a much smaller job than sorting everything on the off-chance.

## What happens next

You send the photo. We come back with a real cash number — not a range designed to be negotiated down at the door, and not a promise we adjust once we've seen it. If the number works for you, we arrange a time and we come to you. We do the lifting and the loading.

If the number doesn't work for you, that's a completely fine outcome and there's no follow-up pressure. You'll have found out what it's worth, which is worth knowing either way.

## The bins we see most

If you're wondering whether your particular pile counts, the honest answer is that it probably does. The things we most often get sent photos of:

- Consoles and handhelds from any era, working or not
- Loose cartridges and discs, including the common ones
- Controllers, cables, memory cards, the drawer of accessories
- Old toys and action figures — boxed, loose, incomplete, whole bins of them
- The tub that got moved from one house to the next and never opened

## Just send the photo

Whatever it is, it's been sitting there long enough. Take one picture from where you're standing and send it over — we'll take it from there.
