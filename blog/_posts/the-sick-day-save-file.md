# The Sick-Day Save File

There was school-sick, and there was sick-sick, and every kid in America knew the difference by second grade.

Sick-sick was real. Nobody's nostalgic about sick-sick.

School-sick was the other thing: the scratchy throat that arrived, with suspicious timing, on the morning of the spelling test. The thermometer read by a skeptical parent in bad light. The verdict — *fine, but you're staying in bed* — delivered like a sentence, received like a lottery win.

And then the door closed, the car pulled out, and the greatest recovery program in medical history began.

## The setup

It assembled itself in minutes, every time, like a pit crew: the blanket relocated from bed to couch (crucial distinction — the couch was the sanctioned sick bay). The tissue box, positioned for visibility more than use. The ginger ale, poured over ice like medicine. And the console, warmed up before the garage door had finished closing.

By 9:15 AM you were under the blanket, controller in hand, embarking on the kind of gaming session the weekend could never provide: **unlimited, unsupervised, and morally licensed.** You weren't goofing off. You were *recovering.*

## Eight hours of medicine

The sick day had a texture no Saturday could match. Saturdays had chores lurking, siblings negotiating for turns, daylight making claims on you. The sick day had none of it — just the long, uninterrupted middle of a game you could finally sink into. Whole worlds got finished on sick days. Save files jumped forward by eras. The hardest boss in the game fell at 1:40 PM on a Tuesday to a kid in pajamas with a blanket cape, and no one witnessed it, and that was somehow part of the glory.

One rule governed everything: **the recovery had to be plausible by 3:30.** The blanket returned to a convincing arrangement. The console cooled. The face rehearsed its wanness. And the acting performance at the front door — weak smile, small voice, "a little better, I think" — deserved more awards than it ever received. Some parents were fooled. Most, we now understand, were choosing mercy.

## The save file remembers

Somewhere in a closet right now, there's a memory card holding the evidence — a save file whose timestamp is a school-day Tuesday, 2:47 PM, that nobody ever questioned. The consoles that hosted those recoveries are sitting in tubs all over this metro, and honestly, they earned the rest.

Today, just the testimony: **what did you play on sick days — and did the acting hold up when mom got home?** Full confessions welcome. She probably knew. 🤒
