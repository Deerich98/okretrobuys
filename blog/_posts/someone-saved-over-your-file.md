# Someone Saved Over Your File

There are childhood injustices you get over. The bike that got stolen. The trading cards your mom donated. And then there's the one a certain generation still brings up at Thanksgiving, voice perfectly level, eyes not level at all: *someone saved over the file.*

Sixty-one hours. Every side quest cleared. The save parked deliberately right before the final boss — not finished, because finishing would end it, but *staged*, like an heirloom on a shelf. And then one ordinary afternoon the TV comes on, the menu loads, and where your life's work used to be, the screen says the two cruelest words in the language: **NEW GAME**.

## The social contract of three slots

The save-select screen was a legal document everybody in the house had signed without reading. Slot one belonged to whoever got the game first — squatters' rights, never contested. Slot three was the guest slot, the communal scratch pad, the demilitarized zone. And slot two — slot two was where tragedy lived. Slot two was *technically* yours, but "technically" is doing heavy lifting in any household with more than one player and only three slots.

The person who did the deed always has the same defense, delivered with the same shrug: "It said the slot was empty." It did not say the slot was empty. No slot with sixty-one hours on it has ever looked empty. What happened is they wanted to try the game, the first two slots had numbers on them, and five minutes of *their* curiosity was worth a summer of *your* progress. They were seven. It was still wrong.

## The aftermath

Every household that suffered a save-over developed law afterward. Handwritten signs taped to the console. Memory cards that started traveling to school in backpacks, like state secrets. The solemn family meeting where slot assignments were formally ratified. One kid in every neighborhood kept their memory card in a sock drawer under actual socks, and honestly? That kid was right.

And the strange thing is what the injustice preserved. Ask anyone about their lost file and you'll get specifics — the hour count, the boss they never fought, exactly who did it and what excuse they gave. Nobody remembers the files they finished. The save that got deleted is the one that became permanent.

## The consoles remember too

Here's the buy-side footnote to all this: those machines are still out there, in closets and under TVs all over the metro, and plenty of them still hold somebody's ancient saves — three slots of 2003 frozen mid-quest. When old consoles and memory cards come our way, that's part of what's riding along: the physical object where the summer went. If you've got one gathering dust — the console, the games, the memory card in the sock drawer — you know where to find us. One photo, a real cash number, OKC metro.

But first, the important business: who did it at your house? Name them in the comments. And the harder question, the one that separates the healed from the merely quiet — have you *truly* forgiven them?
