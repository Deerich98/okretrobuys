Every week somebody messages us a photo of a game shelf with the same question attached: *is any of this worth anything?*

The honest answer is that it's almost never what people expect. The game everyone assumes is valuable usually isn't, and the boring-looking one in the back frequently is. Here's the plain-English version of what actually carries value — so you know what you're sitting on before you decide what to do with it.

## What usually carries real value

- **Complete-in-box anything.** A game with its case, its manual and its inserts is worth multiples of the loose disc or cartridge. That flimsy paper manual you almost threw away is often half the value.
- **Boxed consoles.** Same rule, bigger numbers. A console still in the box it came in — foam, cables, styrofoam and all — is in a different league from a loose one.
- **Later-run games on a dying system.** Games released at the very end of a console's life had short print runs. There aren't many, and that's the whole story.
- **RPGs, niche genres and small-publisher releases.** They sold in smaller numbers when new, and the people who loved them still want them.
- **Handhelds, and especially handheld accessories.** Chargers, link cables, original cases and screen covers get lost. Loose accessories are worth more than people think.
- **Anything sealed.** Doesn't matter what it is. Sealed is its own category.

## What usually isn't the jackpot

- **Sports titles.** A new one came out every year and everyone bought it. There are a lot of them.
- **The pack-in game.** Whatever came bundled with the console shipped in enormous numbers.
- **Best-sellers everybody owned.** The most beloved games are frequently the most common ones. Popular and rare are different things.
- **Discs with heavy scratches**, cartridges with corroded contacts, and anything with water damage.

## The stuff people leave out of the photo

This is the part worth reading twice, because it's where money gets thrown away:

**Cables and controllers.** Original first-party controllers hold value, and the correct power supply or AV cable for an older system is often the hardest part to replace. Photograph the tangle in the bottom of the box too.

**Memory cards, adapters, light guns, multitaps.** Odd accessories are exactly the things that got lost, which is exactly why people want them.

**The empty cases.** Even a case with no game has value to somebody rebuilding a complete copy.

**The manuals in the drawer.** Seriously.
