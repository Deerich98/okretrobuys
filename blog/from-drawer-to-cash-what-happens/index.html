# From Drawer To Cash: What Actually Happens

The three steps in today's video sound almost suspiciously simple — one photo, send it over, cash in hand. Fair enough. Here's the honest, slightly longer version of each step, so you know exactly what you're signing up for before you open the drawer.

Step one: the photo needs less than you think

People apologize for their photos constantly, and there's no need. We're not judging composition — we're identifying objects. One picture of the shelf, the stack, or the whole drawer exactly as it sits usually tells us most of what we need: what systems are represented, roughly how many games, what condition the cases are in, whether there's hardware in the mix.

Things you genuinely don't need to do first: sort by console, untangle the cords, test whether anything powers on, dust anything, or separate "the good stuff." The pile in its natural state is fine. If we need a closer look at something — a particular cartridge, the back of a console — we'll just ask for one more picture of that spot.

Step two: what we look at when we make a number

When the photo comes in, we're reading a few things at once: which titles jump out (there are always two or three doing more work than the rest of the drawer combined), what the hardware is and whether the cables that matter are visible, and how the lot rounds out — manuals, memory cards, the odd accessory riding along.

Then we come back with a real cash number for the lot. Not a range that shrinks in person, not "bring it in and we'll see." A number. If something in the photo could swing it — a game whose condition really matters, say — we'll tell you that up front rather than surprise you at the handoff.

And if the honest answer is that your lot isn't a fit for us, we'll say that too, quickly, so you're not left waiting.

Step three: the handoff, your way

Some folks want us to come to them; some would much rather meet somewhere. Both are normal and both are fine — we pick up or meet, whichever you prefer. Daylight, a public spot, bring a friend, whatever makes it comfortable. The exchange itself takes about as long as reading this sentence: you hand over the lot, we hand over the cash, everyone confirms they're happy, done.

That's the whole machine. No consignment window, no waiting for your stuff to sell, no fees appearing at the end. The drawer's been closed on that collection for years anyway — this is just the three-step version of finally opening it. OKC metro, whenever you're ready. 🎮
