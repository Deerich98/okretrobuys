# Summer Had A Deadline

Adults think of summer as a season. Kids know better. Summer is a budget — a finite number of unscheduled days, spendable exactly once — and every August, a certain kind of kid ran the same grim accounting: days left until school, measured against worlds left until the final boss.

Because summer games had a deadline, and everybody knew it.

The August math

June was infinite. You started something enormous in June — the sprawling adventure, the hundred-hour quest — with the reckless confidence of someone who believes the calendar works for them. July was cruising altitude: real progress, side quests indulged, secrets hunted, no clock anywhere in sight.

Then August arrived and the math got loud. School supply displays went up — the back-to-school aisle appearing in stores like frost on a window. Practices started. Bedtimes, dormant since May, began negotiating their return. And somewhere in every house, a kid looked at a save file, looked at a calendar, and understood with total clarity: it's the game or it isn't, and there are eleven days left.

The final sprint

The last week of summer produced a specific kind of gaming: urgent, focused, borderline professional. Breakfast eaten in front of the menu screen. The strategy guide (the good one, highlighted) open on the carpet. Younger siblings deputized to hold things, look things up, and be quiet. Every unnecessary side activity abandoned — no time to catch them all, no time for the optional dungeon, straight-line it to the end.

Because everyone knew the alternative. A game not beaten by the first bell wasn't paused — it was archived. Homework would eat the weekdays, practice would eat the afternoons, and the save file would sit there untouched until Christmas break, an open tab in a kid's soul. Some games came back at Christmas and got finished in a triumphant three-day blitz. Some never came back at all. There are people in this metro, functioning adults with mortgages, still quietly mad about a summer that ended one castle short. If that's you: it wasn't your fault. The calendar was rigged.

Where the deadline games went

Here's the thing about the games that beat the bell and the ones that didn't: they all ended up in the same place. The shelf, then the closet, then the tub in the garage — the whole history of those Augusts, boxed up together. The triumphs and the unfinished business, side by side, waiting out the decades.

If this week's back-to-school quiet has you eyeing that tub: one photo of it as it sits, and we'll come back with a real cash number. OKC metro, we pick up or meet, no rush ever. The save files are long gone, but the games still count for something.

First, though, the comments: which game did summer end on for you? And the follow-up that matters — did you ever finish it, or is it still waiting for a Christmas break that never came?
